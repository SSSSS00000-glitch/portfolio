Product experience

Rethink the core experience

Users saw neurofeedback as the future of training their brain. That came with high expectations: the experience needed to feel as advanced as the technology behind it. I focused on bringing that expectation into the core experience, making training feel more intuitive, responsive and distinctly Mendi.

A note on confidentiality

This case study focuses on my process, design decisions and selected outcomes. Some internal product metrics, research data and business information have been omitted or generalised.

The training tab

The Training tab was substantially redesigned to create clearer hierarchy around the different ways users can train and introduce the calmer visual direction.

Original Dense, score-led experience
1st iteration Clarifying hierarchy and the next action forward
2nd iteration A calmer, more structured view of performance and progress
Key feature

Device status

Rather than adding more information to an already dense Training screen, I surfaced device status only when it became relevant, alerting users when the headset needs attention, with more detail available on demand.

Low battery alert Prompts users when the headset needs charging.
Device details Device status stays accessible without adding permanent UI.
Key feature

Mendi Score

The Mendi Score combines several aspects of training into one number. I introduced contextual explanations directly from the score, helping users understand what their result means, how it is calculated, and why it may change over time.

Device details Device status stays accessible without adding permanent UI.
Score insights Shows what the current score means.

The brain training

The session experience evolved from a simple start screen into a more flexible setup. I explored different ways to surface guidance and settings, ultimately bringing training options into one place so users could more easily discover, personalise and shape each session.

Original Settings were easy to miss
1st iteration Bringing more guidance into the session setup
Current direction Making training options easier to discover and explore
Experience improvements

Session preparation

First-time users overlooked the original info icon in the top-right corner, but still needed guidance on using the headband. I brought essential preparation into the session flow, keeping it lightweight and reducing the steps needed to get started.

Session Outro

The session outro transitions users from landscape training back into the portrait app. I used this moment to reinforce their routine, showing weekly progress before they continue.

Rethinking the Profile tab

The existing Profile tab primarily functioned as a collection of settings and utilities. I explored moving these into a dedicated Settings area, creating a clearer home for account and training preferences while allowing the Profile experience to evolve in a new direction.
